About

I am a Professor of Economics at Musashi University in Tokyo. I received my Ph.D. in Economics from the University of Wisconsin–Madison. My current research develops machine learning methods that go beyond prediction to answer structural questions in economics—identifying regime shifts that traditional econometrics cannot detect, building diagnostic protocols that break through identification barriers, and constructing computational platforms for behavioral experiments.

Two complementary research lines: (1) ML interpretability for structural decomposition—using SHAP and neural network architectures to isolate components that time fixed effects absorb; (2) LLMs as experimental platforms—developing protocols that transform language models from reasoning tools into instruments for hypothesis testing, behavioral simulation, and structural parameter recovery.

Working Papers

Who Listens to the Market? Macro Conditions and Stock Market Participation Across the Wealth Distribution Working Paper
Ching-Yang Lin, Zin Mar Oo, Makoto Kakinaka · 2026 · Working Paper
Macroeconomic conditions account for 17–21% of the prediction signal for stock participation. The effect concentrates sharply in the upper-middle wealth range: counterfactual macro swaps shift predicted participation by 33 percentage points for deciles 8–9, but only 4pp for the poorest quintile.
Flood Risk Capitalization Under Evolving Information Regimes: Evidence from Tokyo’s Real Estate Market Working Paper
Ching-Yang Lin, Yuen Leng Chow · 2026 · Working Paper
Passive information provision (online hazard maps, interactive overlays) has zero detectable effect on flood risk pricing; only mandatory disclosure and disaster salience drive market awareness, with a true flood discount of −18.1% to −12.2% depending on property type.
Specification-Free Detection of Structural Regime Shifts via LSTM Autoencoder Working Paper
Ching-Yang Lin, Makoto Kakinaka · 2026 · Working Paper
An LSTM autoencoder with per-variable reconstruction error achieves 93% detection without specifying the data-generating process; Bai–Perron with the correct regression reaches 63%.
